# ISO/IEC 42001:2023: code review checklist

Source: ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Information technology, Artificial intelligence, Management system, Annex A (reference controls for AI systems), https://www.iso.org/standard/42001. The standard is © ISO and is sold by ISO, so the checks below are written in our own words and cite Annex A control numbers only. The IDs are the Annex A control numbers, so a finding can be traced back to the official text.

ISO/IEC 42001 is a management system standard. Most of it is about policies, roles and records that are not in code. The checks below are the Annex A controls whose evidence can show up in code, configuration, data pipelines and CI/CD.

## When this applies

Apply these checks only to changed code that builds, trains, evaluates, deploys, runs or monitors an AI system. This includes model calls, prompts, training and evaluation scripts, data pipelines for training data, model packaging and release pipelines, logging and monitoring of AI behaviour, and user-facing AI features.

If the change doesn't touch any of these, report this standard as `not_applicable`.

## Checks

### [ISO 42001 A.4.3] The data an AI system uses is identified and recorded
Look for: new datasets, tables, feeds or files used for training, tuning, grounding or evaluation with no note of where they come from and what they hold, in a project that keeps such records (data cards, dataset manifests).
Decided from code: partly. The records may be kept outside the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.4.4] AI tools and libraries are known and controlled
Look for: new ML frameworks, model runtimes, model-serving tools or AI SDKs added with no pinned version, or from sources other than the project's usual ones.
Decided from code: yes, for dependency and build files.

### [ISO 42001 A.4.5] System and computing resources for the AI system are defined
Look for: model serving or training jobs deployed with no resource limits, no quota or budget guard, or no timeout, where the project sets them for similar jobs. Infrastructure files that remove limits or redundancy for the AI service.
Decided from code: partly. Capacity planning is mostly outside the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.1.3] The AI system is designed and built through a defined process
Look for: model or prompt changes that skip the project's usual pipeline, such as no review, no evaluation step, or direct edits of deployed model or prompt files. Release steps for AI components that are removed or bypassed.
Decided from code: partly. The process itself is often documented elsewhere.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.2] The requirements and intended behaviour of the AI system are written down
Look for: a new AI feature or model with no stated purpose, inputs, outputs or limits in the project's usual place (README, model card, design doc, config), where similar features have one.
Decided from code: partly. A requirements document may be outside the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.3] Design and development choices are recorded
Look for: changes of model, base model, training method, features, thresholds or prompts with no record of the reason, in a project that keeps one (changelog, model card, experiment log).
Decided from code: partly.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.4] The AI system is verified and validated before release
Look for: model, prompt or retrieval changes with no new or updated evaluation: no test set, no metric, no regression check, or evaluation steps removed, skipped or loosened (lower thresholds) in CI. Tests with the expected values copied from the model's own output.
Decided from code: yes.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.5] Deployment of AI changes follows a controlled plan
Look for: model or prompt versions deployed with no version pin, so that a provider's change can alter behaviour silently (`latest` model aliases). No staged rollout, rollback path or fallback where the project has one. Models loaded from unverified locations at start-up.
Decided from code: yes, for deployment files in the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.6] The AI system is monitored while it runs
Look for: removal of monitoring, quality or drift checks, error and refusal tracking, or alerts on AI behaviour. New AI features with no metrics, no feedback capture and no failure handling, where similar features have them.
Decided from code: partly. Dashboards and alert rules may live outside the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.7] Technical documentation of the AI system stays current
Look for: changes to the model, its inputs, limits or dependencies that leave the model card, architecture note or technical documentation in the repository out of date.
Decided from code: partly. Only documentation kept in the repository can be seen.

### [ISO 42001 A.6.2.8] The AI system records event logs
Look for: removal of logging of AI requests, model and prompt versions, outputs, tool calls, overrides and errors. Logs that cannot link an output to the model version that produced it. Logs that keep raw personal data without need.
Decided from code: yes.

### [ISO 42001 A.7.3] Data is acquired from allowed sources, with a record of how
Look for: scraping, bulk download or import of third-party data for training with no recorded source, licence or permission, and data sources added with no reference to the project's rules for acquiring data.
Decided from code: partly. Licence and consent status may be tracked elsewhere.

### [ISO 42001 A.7.4] The quality of data used for AI is checked
Look for: training or evaluation data pipelines with no validation (schema, ranges, duplicates, missing values, label checks), validation removed, or known-bad records let through. Test data that overlaps with training data.
Decided from code: yes, for data pipeline code.

### [ISO 42001 A.7.5] The origin and history of data is traceable
Look for: dataset versions that are not pinned or recorded, transformations that drop source identifiers, and training runs that can't be linked to the exact data and code used (no dataset hash, no run metadata).
Decided from code: yes, for pipeline code.

### [ISO 42001 A.7.6] Data preparation is done in a defined, repeatable way
Look for: cleaning, labelling, filtering or augmentation done by ad hoc scripts or manual steps with no committed code, or with settings that are not recorded. Preparation that removes safeguards, such as dropping the filter for personal data or unsafe content.
Decided from code: yes.

### [ISO 42001 A.8.2] Users get the information they need about the AI system
Look for: user-facing AI features that stop saying what the system does, its limits, and that output may be wrong or AI-generated. Removal of in-product help, labels or warnings for AI output.
Decided from code: yes, for text and UI in the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.8.4] Incidents with the AI system can be reported and communicated
Look for: removal of error or incident reporting hooks, a feedback or "report a problem" path for AI output, or a contact point for AI incidents, where the product has them. Failures of the AI component swallowed with no signal.
Decided from code: partly. The incident process is mostly outside the repository.

### [ISO 42001 A.9.4] The AI system is used only as intended
Look for: new uses of an existing model or endpoint outside its stated purpose, with no guard. Removal of checks that limit the system to its intended inputs, users or contexts, such as scope checks, input filters or usage restrictions.
Decided from code: partly. Intended use is defined outside the code.

### [ISO 42001 A.10.3] AI suppliers and third-party AI services are controlled
Look for: new third-party model APIs, pretrained models, datasets or AI libraries with no review of the provider, no pinned model version, no contract-related note where the project keeps one, and personal data sent to a new provider with no basis recorded.
Decided from code: partly. Supplier review records may be outside the repository.
